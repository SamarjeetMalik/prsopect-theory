Reward-design comparisons in cooperative reinforcement learning ask whether a transform of the reward changes what agents learn. We measure this along a local/team reward-weighting dial by an operational, finite-budget crossing $w_{50}$, the weight at which a fitted probability of a pre-specified low-cooperation outcome passes 50\%. A symmetric rescaling of the reward should not change what is learned, yet in default Stable-Baselines3 PPO on an inventory network, scaling both reward branches by seven moves the crossing by \VjtPStd{}, and a CleanRL-style implementation reproduces the shift (\VjtPElevenRaw{}). We had pre-registered a mechanism: actor and critic share one gradient-norm clip, the unnormalized critic's gradient dominates it, and the actor's update shrinks. That distortion is real, since the actor's applied gradient falls to \VsixGradGlobal{} of baseline and its Adam step to \VxoGlobStep{}, but it is not shown to produce the shift. On fresh seeds in SB3, clipping actor and critic separately restores the actor's step without reducing the shift (change \VxFCh{}; in the CleanRL-style implementation a small reduction appears only when cohorts are pooled); in a pre-registered actor-by-critic clipping factorial neither group's clip has a detectable effect, and the shift persists with no clipping at all (\VxtNeither{}), although in the earlier fresh cohort the pre-registered no-clip shift was not detectable (\VxFNone{}). In an exploratory pool of these forty fresh seeds (82--121) the shift is \VxeGroup{} with per-group clipping and \VxeNone{} without, so clipping may enlarge it, by an amount that differs between the two cohorts. With normalized rewards no symmetric shift is detectable in either implementation (\VjtPSymBoth{}; \VjtPElevenDefault{}), and, pooled over three cohorts (seeds 42--81), normalizing only the critic's targets reduces it (change \VjtChPool{}). In every clipping mode tested the critic fits the scaled returns worse (in X3, explained variance lower by \VxtEVLo{}--\VxtEVHi{}). On forty further fresh seeds a pre-registered test locates the shift, at 300k and without clipping, in the scale of the critic's regression targets: with the plain critic the actor's advantages are measurably worse under scaling, while a critic whose output is fixed-scaled by the multiplier, so that it regresses targets of the baseline's size (\VxfHead{}), or a PopArt critic (\VxfPop{}) equalizes the fit and leaves a shift equivalent to zero within $\pm0.10$. Both interventions remove the target scale and equalize the fit together, so the critic's fit is a candidate mediator, not an isolated one. Intervals are seed-level jackknife-$t$ intervals, which a simulation study of the design found near nominal where the pre-declared bootstrap under-covers. With rewards normalized and no clipping (SB3), a loss-only multiplier still moves the crossing relative to the symmetric control (\VjtPAsymBoth{}), through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions; against a symmetric control matched online in training-reward mean and SD, the effect is large when the critic's targets are normalized (\VjtPAMonPop{}) and small with raw targets (\VjtPAMonPlain{}, which does not survive a Holm adjustment). The symmetric shift is measured in one environment at 300k transitions, and except where stated with a fully observed, parameter-shared controller. On forty more fresh seeds, again without clipping, it replicates when each node has its own actor acting on local observations with a centralized critic (\VxvPlain{}), and both critics again remove it; with a shared actor and critic both on local views, under the shared clip and with ten seeds, it was not detectable, which is inconclusive. Reward-transform comparisons with an unnormalized critic should include a symmetric-scaling control. $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument, not a critical point.
