Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning can collapse into low-cooperation behavior when local incentives outweigh the team objective. We define an operational estimand for where this happens: the finite-budget crossing $w_{50}$ along a local/team reward-weighting dial at which collapse becomes more likely than not, estimated from behavioral labels with training-seed-block bootstrap intervals. Asking whether a loss-asymmetric reward transform moves it, we find the answer depends on optimizer coupling. In default Stable-Baselines3 PPO on an inventory network, actor and critic share one gradient clip that the critic dominates; scaling both reward branches by seven moves the crossing by \VfourSymStd{} and shrinks the actor's parameter updates to \VsixUpdGlobal{} of baseline. Clipping actor and critic separately restores the updates and removes part of the shift (\VsixSymGroupChange{}); reward normalization removes the detectable remainder; normalizing only the critic's targets shrinks it by an amount we cannot distinguish from zero (\VsevenSymChange{}). With separate optimizers and normalized rewards, where no symmetric shift is detectable (\VfourSymBoth{}), a loss-only multiplier still moves the crossing relative to the symmetric control (\VfourAsymBoth{}), through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions (\VfiveAsymEnd{}). This contrast does not fix the reward's moments. With raw rewards and a critic regressing raw returns, a symmetric control matched to the loss-only arm's reward scale (though not its mean) moves the crossing nearly as far (difference \VsevenAMPlain{}), so there the effect may be one of reward moments; when only the critic's targets are normalized, the loss-only arm exceeds that control (\VsevenAMPop{}). In Level-Based Foraging an asymmetric transform also moves the crossing, there by penalizing a cooperative action. Crossings stay budget- and configuration-dependent: $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument, not a critical point.
