Cooperative multi-agent learners can collapse into low-cooperation behavior when local incentives outweigh the team objective, and reward design is a common remedy. We measure where collapse sets in along a local/team reward-weighting dial by an operational, finite-budget crossing $w_{50}$, the weight at which a fitted collapse probability passes 50\%, estimated from behavioral labels with training-seed-block intervals. A symmetric rescaling of the reward should not change what is learned, yet in default Stable-Baselines3 PPO on an inventory network, scaling both reward branches by seven moves the crossing by \VfourSymStd{}. The cause is an implementation detail: actor and critic share one global gradient-norm clip, the unnormalized critic's gradient dominates it, and the actor's applied gradient and parameter update shrink to \VsixGradGlobal{} and \VsixUpdGlobal{} of baseline. Clipping actor and critic separately restores the update and reduces the shift by \VsixSymGroupChange{}, and raising the clip threshold shrinks it gradually. A second, CleanRL-style PPO implementation reproduces the shift (\VelevenSymRaw{}). Reward normalization leaves no detectable symmetric shift in either implementation (\VfourSymBoth{}; \VelevenSymDefault{}), and in two fresh-seed cohorts normalizing only the critic's targets also removes the remainder (changes \VeightBChange{} and \VtenChange{}; each holds at the declared 95\% level but not at a coverage-calibrated one unless cohorts are pooled). With the coupling removed, a loss-only multiplier still moves the crossing relative to the symmetric control (\VfourAsymBoth{}), through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions. This asymmetric effect survives a symmetric control whose training-reward mean and SD are matched online to the loss-only arm's, but its size depends on the critic: it is large when the critic's targets are normalized (\VeightAMonPop{}) and small with raw targets (\VeightAMonPlain{}), where a moment-matched symmetric reward reproduces most of the loss-only shift. Results come from two small environments and two PPO implementations. With local observations and agent identity the asymmetric effect holds, but the symmetric shift is no longer detectable. $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument, not a critical point.
