Reward-design comparisons in cooperative reinforcement learning ask whether a transform of the reward changes what agents learn. We measure this along a local/team reward-weighting dial by an operational, finite-budget crossing $w_{50}$, the weight at which a fitted probability of a pre-specified low-cooperation outcome passes 50\%. A symmetric rescaling of the reward should not change what is learned, yet in default Stable-Baselines3 PPO on an inventory network, scaling both reward branches by seven moves the crossing by \VjtPStd{}, and a CleanRL-style implementation reproduces the shift (\VjtPElevenRaw{}). We had pre-registered a mechanism: actor and critic share one gradient-norm clip, the unnormalized critic's gradient dominates it, and the actor's update shrinks. That distortion is real, since the actor's applied gradient falls to \VsixGradGlobal{} of baseline and its Adam step to \VxoGlobStep{}, but the shift does not need it. On fresh seeds in SB3, clipping actor and critic separately restores the actor's step without reducing the shift (change \VxFCh{}; in the CleanRL-style implementation a small reduction appears only when cohorts are pooled); in a pre-registered actor-by-critic clipping factorial neither group's clip has a detectable effect, and the shift persists with no clipping at all (\VxtNeither{}). Pooled over the forty fresh seeds, an exploratory analysis, clipping enlarges the shift (\VxeGroup{} against \VxeNone{} without clipping), so the clip may add to it but is not its source. Normalization is what removes it: with normalized rewards no symmetric shift is detectable in either implementation (\VjtPSymBoth{}; \VjtPElevenDefault{}), and, pooled over forty seeds, normalizing only the critic's targets reduces it (change \VjtChPool{}). In every clipping mode the critic fits the scaled returns worse (explained variance lower by \VxtEVLo{}). Intervals are seed-level jackknife-$t$ intervals, which a simulation study of the design found near nominal where the pre-declared bootstrap under-covers. With rewards normalized, a loss-only multiplier still moves the crossing relative to the symmetric control (\VjtPAsymBoth{}), through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions; against a symmetric control matched online in training-reward mean and SD, the effect is large when the critic's targets are normalized (\VjtPAMonPop{}) and small with raw targets (\VjtPAMonPlain{}, which does not survive a Holm adjustment). Results come from two small environments and two PPO implementations, mostly with a fully observed shared controller; under local observation with agent identity the symmetric shift is not detectable. Reward-transform comparisons with an unnormalized critic should include a symmetric-scaling control. $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument, not a critical point.
