Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning can fail when local incentives outweigh the team objective. We introduce a protocol for measuring where this failure occurs as the reward mixture changes, locating the \AbstractCrossingProbability{} collapse crossing $w_{50}$ from behavioral collapse labels with seed-block uncertainty, and use it to test whether a loss-asymmetric reward transform moves the crossing. On an inventory network with shared-parameter PPO, a pre-registered optimizer $\times$ reward-normalization factorial (1,600 trajectories) shows that the answer depends on an implementation detail. In the default Stable-Baselines3 configuration the critic dominates a global gradient clip shared with the actor, so any reward scaling throttles the actor: a symmetric multiplier moves the crossing as far as a loss-only multiplier (\VfourSymStd{}). With separate optimizers and normalized rewards the symmetric effect vanishes (\VfourSymBoth{}) while the loss-only multiplier still raises the crossing, giving an asymmetric-minus-symmetric contrast of \VfourAsymBoth{}; the artifact acts through critic target magnitude, not Adam's $\epsilon$. The asymmetric contrast replicates with centralized-critic MAPPO on Level-Based Foraging under both optimizer modes (\VfourLBFAsymSep{} with separate optimizers), where the multiplier raises the effort cost of cooperating. Under the default optimizer, a refined horizon cohort shows that crossings remain budget-dependent to \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions and that collapse is not absorbing. Absolute crossings depend on the optimizer configuration as well as on the reward design, so $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument whose readings must be reported with the learner, budget, reward units, optimizer and label.
