Cooperative multi-agent learners can collapse into low-cooperation behavior when local incentives outweigh the team objective, and reward design is a common remedy. We measure where collapse sets in along a local/team reward-weighting dial by an operational, finite-budget crossing $w_{50}$, the weight at which a fitted collapse probability passes 50\%, estimated from behavioral labels with training-seed-block intervals. A symmetric rescaling of the reward should not change what is learned, yet in default Stable-Baselines3 PPO on an inventory network, scaling both reward branches by seven moves the crossing by \VfourSymStd{}. The cause is an implementation detail: actor and critic share one global gradient-norm clip, the unnormalized critic's gradient dominates it, and the actor's applied gradient and parameter update shrink to \VsixGradGlobal{} and \VsixUpdGlobal{} of baseline. Clipping actor and critic separately restores the update and reduces the shift by \VsixSymGroupChange{}; reward normalization leaves no detectable symmetric shift (\VfourSymBoth{}), and normalizing only the critic's targets shrinks the remainder by an amount ten seeds cannot resolve. With the coupling removed, a loss-only multiplier still moves the crossing relative to the symmetric control (\VfourAsymBoth{}), through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions. This asymmetric finding is conditional: against a symmetric control matched to its reward scale but not its mean, it is separable only when the critic's targets are normalized (\VsevenAMPop{}), not with raw targets (\VsevenAMPlain{}). Results come from two small environments and one PPO implementation; $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument, not a critical point.
