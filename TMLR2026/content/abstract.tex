Reward-design comparisons in cooperative reinforcement learning ask whether a transform of the reward changes what agents learn. We measure this along a local/team reward-weighting dial by an operational, finite-budget crossing $w_{50}$, the weight at which a fitted probability of a pre-specified low-cooperation outcome passes 50\%. A symmetric rescaling of the reward should not change what is learned, yet in default Stable-Baselines3 PPO on an inventory network, scaling both reward branches by seven moves the crossing by \VjtPStd{}, about as far as a loss-only multiplier, and a CleanRL-style implementation reproduces the shift (\VjtPElevenRaw{}). With normalized rewards it is not detectable in either implementation (\VjtPSymBoth{}; \VjtPElevenDefault{}). Without clipping a smaller shift remains, and it follows the scale of the critic's regression targets. In a pre-registered test on forty fresh seeds without clipping, the unnormalized critic shows a shift of \VxfPlain{}, fits the scaled returns worse and builds worse advantages, while two critics that remove the target scale (a head whose output is fixed-scaled by the multiplier, and a PopArt critic) leave shifts of \VxfHead{} and \VxfPop{}, both equivalent to zero within $\pm0.10$. The result repeats on forty more seeds when each node has its own actor acting on local observations, with a centralized critic (plain critic \VxvPlain{}; fixed-scale head \VxvHead{}; PopArt \VxvPop{}). Both interventions also equalize the critic's fit. Slowing the baseline's critic to the scaled arm's fit, at the baseline's target scale, reproduces the shift (\VxeiDeg{} against \VxeiPlain{}), so a critic slowed to that fit is sufficient to produce it on the shared controller without clipping; this is one degradation and does not show that fit mediates the effect of reward scale. With per-node local critics the shift is present (\VxsixPlain{}), but the fixed-scale head's residual (\VxsixHead{}) misses the equivalence margin, so the channel there is unresolved. The mechanism we had pre-registered, the shared actor--critic gradient clip starving the actor, is real (the actor's Adam step falls to \VxoGlobStep{} of baseline) but is not shown to move the crossing: on fresh seeds, clipping actor and critic separately restores the step and leaves the shift. With per-node actors under the shared clip the shift is smaller (\VxsevPlain{}); critics with baseline-sized targets and gradients remove it (\VxsevHead{}; \VxsevPop{}), and per-group clipping does not detectably change it (\VxsevChGroup{}). How much of the default shared controller's larger shift runs through the critic's targets is not tested. Intervals are seed-level jackknife-$t$ intervals, which a simulation of the design found near nominal where the pre-declared bootstrap under-covers. A loss-only multiplier's own effect depends on the normalization regime. With rewards normalized and no clipping it raises the crossing relative to the symmetric control (\VjtPAsymBoth{}), through \MaxConfirmationBudget{} transitions. With raw rewards and no clipping, against a symmetric control matched online in training-reward mean and SD, it is large when the critic's targets are normalized (\VjtPAMonPop{}) and small when they are not (\VjtPAMonPlain{}, which does not survive a Holm adjustment). The results are for one environment at 300k transitions and, except where stated, a fully observed, parameter-shared controller; with a shared actor and critic both on local views, under the shared clip and with ten seeds, the shift was not detected, which is inconclusive. Reward-transform comparisons with an unnormalized critic should include a symmetric-scaling control. $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument, not a critical point.
