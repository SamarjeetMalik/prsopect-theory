Cooperative multi-agent reinforcement learning can fail when local incentives outweigh the team objective. We introduce a protocol for measuring where this failure occurs as the reward mixture changes, locating the \AbstractCrossingProbability{} collapse crossing $w_{50}$ from behavioral collapse labels with seed-block uncertainty. On an inventory-network testbed with shared-parameter PPO the crossing is \AbstractCrossing{} at \PrimaryBudget{} agent transitions, and a loss-branch multiplier moves it by \LossMultiplierShift{} relative to a curvature-only baseline. In the default Stable-Baselines3 configuration, however, a symmetric multiplier moves the crossing as far (\VfourSymStd{}): the critic dominates a shared global gradient clip, so reward scale throttles the actor. A pre-registered optimizer $\times$ reward-normalisation factorial (1,600 trajectories) removes this artifact. With separate optimizers and normalised rewards the symmetric effect vanishes (\VfourSymBoth{}) while the loss-only multiplier still raises the crossing; the asymmetric-minus-symmetric contrast is \VfourAsymBoth{}. Lowering Adam's $\epsilon$ does not remove the artifact. The asymmetric contrast also holds for centralized-critic MAPPO on Level-Based Foraging under both optimizer modes (\VfourLBFAsymGlobal{} and \VfourLBFAsymSep{}), where the multiplier acts on the effort cost of cooperating. A single-platform refined horizon cohort retains the multiplier-minus-curvature shift, from \VfourHorizonStart{} at 300k to \VfourHorizonEnd{} at \MaxConfirmationBudget{}, but no condition stabilises and collapse is not absorbing. Absolute crossings depend on the optimizer configuration as well as on the reward design, so $w_{50}$ is a comparative instrument whose readings must be reported together with the learner, budget, reward units, optimizer and label.
